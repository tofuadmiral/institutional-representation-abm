# Paper 3: AI representatives, negotiation and delegated authority

This work in progress asks how AI agents can represent citizens in collective decisions while respecting delegated authority. The vision is to identify institutional arrangements that support useful negotiation and cooperation without allowing representatives to override their mandates. Controlled experiments vary accountability rules, officeholder incentives, peer messages and explicit permission constraints.

We have tested coalition accountability, replacement and handover, and negotiation. An early institutional lead failed to replicate, and a matched follow-up did not confirm the initial advocacy effect. The latest diagnostic found that permission-focused review could be followed by unauthorized choices even when the review correctly stated the relevant permissions. That gap between stated constraints and binding action is the current research focus. These results remain exploratory and limited to narrow synthetic tasks, largely with one model; they do not establish intrinsic motives or general safety, and there is no completed Paper 3 manuscript yet.
