The primary paired rejection change from binding bonus 0 to 24 was exactly zero
in all 16 cases. The advisory bonus contrast, interaction, and removal effect at
zero bonus were also zero. There were no discordant primary pairs; the reported
conditional sign diagnostic is p=1, which is not evidence of equivalence or a
population null. With no missing observations, the descriptive missingness bounds
collapse to [0,0]; they are not confidence intervals.

Both binding conditions replaced the acting representative in 16/16 cases.
In the bonus-24 condition, choosing yes gave a district SUM/officeholder score
of 8, 12, 16 or 20 rather than the officeholder score of 24 available by rejecting.
The model thus selected the explicitly entrusted objective despite that stated
score tradeoff. Advisory conditions retained the representative in every case.
Each beneficial proposal raised district SUM by 8–20 (mean 14) and total citizen
utility by 127–164 (mean 150), with zero term-two utility. Each also harmed five
of the acting district's nine citizens. Aggregate benefit is not Pareto improvement
or a claim that majority preferences should be overridden. These are the same
16 cases across four conditions, not 64 independent games. Every individual
utility and district harm count remains in the report. Harmful proposals were
all rejected, leaving zero utility and no realized harms in those controls.

This is evidence of mandate-following in an explicit, fully specified task for
one model. The bonus was described in the prompt, not a real training reward or
stake. The mandate and complete consequences were supplied, and the other six
ballots and all future payoffs were fixed. The result does not identify intrinsic
self-sacrifice, establish general corrigibility/shutdown acceptance, or measure
spontaneous multiagent bargaining. It does not overturn the earlier failed
replication. No further experiment was launched or threshold changed after answers.
